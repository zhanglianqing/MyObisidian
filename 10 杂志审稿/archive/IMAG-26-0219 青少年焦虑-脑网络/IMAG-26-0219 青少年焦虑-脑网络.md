## Review comments（提交 EM · 2026-06-03）

### Overall comment

This manuscript uses data from the ABCD cohort to examine associations between adolescent anxiety and a broad set of neuroimaging, network controllability, graph-theoretical, morphometric, and cognitive measures. The sample is large and valuable, and the clinical question of identifying neural and cognitive correlates of adolescent anxiety is important. However, the manuscript does not currently present this question in a sufficiently focused or compelling way. Although the amount of analysis is substantial, the logic connecting the different analytic components is often unclear. The manuscript moves across structural MRI, resting-state network topology, controllability metrics, cognitive performance, and moderation analyses, but these components are not well integrated into a coherent set of aims or hypotheses. As a result, the study often reads more like an accumulation of analyses than a hypothesis-driven investigation. The overall organization and writing require major revision. Several key sections are especially problematic: the final paragraph of the Introduction does not clearly state the central aims and hypotheses, and the opening paragraph of the Discussion does not clearly summarize the main findings or explain how they advance understanding of adolescent anxiety. In addition, the manuscript contains numerous sentence-level editing problems and an unusually dense reference load, which further obscure the main message. Before the scientific contribution can be fairly evaluated, the authors need to substantially clarify the study rationale, streamline the presentation, and articulate how the major findings relate to the stated clinical and mechanistic questions.

### Specific comments

**1. Abstract and Key Points**

1. Abstract, Introduction: The sentence "Neural substrates and cognitive correlates of adolescent anxiety are incompletely understood, a critical barrier to precise interventions" appears incomplete and overly broad; the study aim and the problem it seeks to address are framed too expansively and should be made more specific.
2. Abstract, Methods: The sentence "The study included n=3499 youth (median(IQR) age =12.0(1.1) years; ~51.0% females)..." contains several nested or adjacent parenthetical elements, which makes it unnecessarily difficult to read; the authors should simplify the wording or split the information into a clearer sentence.
3. Abstract, Results: The sentence "Females and older youth and higher anxiety (β=0.04-1.15, 95%CI=[0.01,2.18])" appears incomplete and unclear; the authors should specify the comparison or association being reported.
4. Key Points: Several statements are too broad or interpretive. In particular, "is harder to control" and "deficits in visuospatial processing" appear to be interpretations of statistical associations rather than directly demonstrated findings; the authors should make the key points more specific and avoid overstating the evidence.

**2. Introduction**

1. Introduction, first paragraph: The opening paragraph is difficult to follow because it combines broad background, prevalence, and multiple categories of risk factors in a densely referenced narrative. The number of citations seems excessive for this general background, and the specific rationale for the present study is not clear after a full page of text; the authors should streamline this section and bring the study motivation forward.
2. Introduction, second paragraph: The paragraph beginning "Anxiety can have detrimental effects on cognitive function..." is more relevant, but it still reads as a broad literature summary rather than a focused rationale for the present analyses; the authors should more clearly state which specific gap in adolescent anxiety, brain networks, and cognition motivates this study.
3. Introduction, second paragraph: The phrase "Pediatric studies in have reported..." appears to contain a grammatical error and should be corrected.
4. Introduction, third paragraph: The discussion of structural alterations that are "common to children and adults" versus "distinct in the two populations" would benefit from a clearer transition to the subsequent gap statement; the implication of these population differences for studying adolescent anxiety specifically could be signposted earlier.
5. Introduction, paragraph beginning "Recent studies using data from the ABCD cohort...": This paragraph is difficult to follow because it lists demographic, environmental, functional connectivity, structural, cognitive, and mechanistic findings in rapid succession. The authors should reorganize this paragraph around a clearer narrative of what ABCD studies have already shown and what remains unknown.
6. Introduction, final paragraph: The sentence beginning "Our and others' work has shown that..." and the following background material would fit better in the preceding literature review rather than in the final paragraph, which should focus on stating the study aim and hypotheses.
7. Introduction, final paragraph: The hypothesis sentence beginning "We hypothesized that anxiety is associated with aberrant topological organization..." has serious structural problems and appears to reflect incomplete editing. As the first hypothesis of the study, this unreadable sentence substantially affects the reader's ability to understand the experimental design and interpret the subsequent results.
8. Introduction, final paragraph: The sentence "we hypothesized that anxiety is associated with changes in the brain's controllability as well, and thus 'costs' of regulating its dynamics..." is not sufficiently clear. The concept of "'costs' of regulating its dynamics" is not adequately introduced here, and the authors should briefly clarify how brain controllability and control costs are operationalized in this study.

**3. Methods**

1. Methods, Section 2.2.1: This section should focus on how anxiety was measured in the present study, including who completed the measure, which scale was used, and whether raw scores, t-scores, or both were analyzed. Details such as which score types were used in prior ABCD studies or whether disorder labels are represented as binary variables in the database are not essential here and could be removed or substantially shortened.
2. Methods, Section 2.2.2: Please clarify what methods were used to harmonize multi-site acquisition effects.
3. Methods, Section 2.2.2: The manuscript mentions "information transfer" as one of the quantities estimated after downsampling to 100 regions, but it is not clear how information transfer was operationalized or whether it was analyzed in the present study. Please clarify or remove this term if it is not part of the current analyses.
4. Methods, Section 2.2.2: The manuscript notes that 76.6% of participants had a second rs-fMRI run of adequate quality, but the role of this second run in the analysis is not clearly described. Please clarify whether it was used for sensitivity, replication, or additional exploratory analyses.
5. Methods, Section 2.7: The FDR correction appears to have been applied within relatively narrow families of tests, such as across topological properties within each network, across regions within each network for each topological property, or across morphometric properties within each anatomical region. Given the large number of anxiety outcomes, brain measures, networks/regions, and moderation models analyzed, the authors should justify these correction families and consider reporting sensitivity analyses using a more conservative multiple-comparison correction strategy.

**4. Results**

1. Results, opening paragraph: The initial paragraph on participant demographic characteristics and anxiety prevalence appears as a standalone subsection before Section 3.1. Please consider adding a subsection heading for this paragraph to make the Results structure clearer.
2. Results/Tables: The table formatting would benefit from revision to better align with standard publication style, such as using cleaner three-line table formatting and reducing unnecessary grid lines.
3. Results, Section 3.1: Several editing issues should be corrected, including "PCF" likely intended as "PFC", the phrase "In contrast, Finally," and "SDA" likely intended as "SAD". In addition, the reported interval "β = -0.07, 95%CI = [-0.92, -0.02]" should be checked, as the lower bound appears unusually large relative to the point estimate.
4. Results, Section 3.2: The network-level sentence beginning "At the network scale..." is difficult to follow because it lists several overlapping networks in a single sentence. Please consider splitting this into clearer statements or listing the networks and corresponding associations more explicitly.
5. Methods/Results, Sections 2.4.2 and 3.2: The meaning and scale of "higher feedback costs" or "higher control costs" are not sufficiently clear for readers who are less familiar with the controllability framework. Please briefly clarify what a higher cost indicates in practical terms, including whether the reported costs were standardized or otherwise normalized before analysis.
6. Results, Section 3.3: Please clarify whether "regional modularity (clustering)" refers to modularity, clustering coefficient, or another regional graph metric, as modularity and clustering are distinct graph-theoretical measures. The final sentence of this section also lists many regions and metrics together and would benefit from being split into clearer statements.
7. Results, Section 3.4: Please clarify whether "white matter hyperintensity" is intended to mean "white matter intensity," as described in Methods Section 2.5. These terms are not equivalent, and "white matter hyperintensity" may be interpreted as referring to WMH lesions or abnormal signal changes.
8. Introduction/Results, Sections 1 and 3.4: The manuscript includes a large number of analyses across structural, topological, dynamic, controllability, and cognitive measures, but the logical relationships among these analysis components are not always clear. In particular, the follow-up analyses relating morphometric and topological characteristics in Section 3.4 would benefit from a clearer explanation of how they connect to the study aims or hypotheses, and whether they were planned or exploratory.
9. Results section numbering: The subsection numbering appears to skip from Section 3.4 to Section 3.6, with no Section 3.5. Please correct the numbering.

**5. Discussion and Conclusions**

1. Discussion, first paragraph: The opening paragraph restates the general rationale and study design but does not clearly summarize the main findings or explain how the study addresses the gap identified in the Introduction. The authors should revise this paragraph to foreground the key results, such as the main structural, topological, controllability, and cognitive findings, and explicitly state what new insight these findings add to the existing literature on adolescent anxiety.
2. Discussion, cerebellum/basal ganglia paragraph: The phrase "provide mechanistic insights" appears stronger than the analyses support, given that the study primarily reports cross-sectional associations between anxiety and neuroimaging metrics. Please consider using more cautious wording unless additional analyses directly test a mechanistic pathway.
3. Discussion, cerebellum/basal ganglia paragraph: The statement that higher control costs may occur "as a result of aberrant connectivity and topological organization" implies a causal or mediating relationship between connectivity/topology and control costs. The reported associations do not appear to directly test this pathway, so this interpretation should be softened or supported with additional analysis.
4. Conclusions: The statement that the study makes an "important scientific contribution" could be revised to more directly summarize the specific contribution of the present findings. The conclusion would be stronger if it briefly restated the most robust results rather than relying on broad evaluative language.
5. Conclusions: The phrase "neural miswiring" is somewhat imprecise and may be too strong for the population-level associations reported here. Please consider using more standard developmental or neurobiological terminology.
6. Conclusions: The statement that the study provides "robust mechanistic insights" appears stronger than warranted by the design and analyses. Since the findings are primarily associative, the authors should use more cautious wording or clarify what specific mechanistic inference is supported.
7. Conclusions: The final sentence suggesting that the identified structures and circuits could become targets of precise intervention is interesting but premature as currently written. Please temper this statement and specify that further longitudinal or interventional work would be needed before translating these associations into intervention targets.
