The current study summarized available published data on amygdala-targeting NF while addressing three issues related to NF feasibility and utility: 

#这是个非常好的框架，与feasibility and utility的定义有关

  1. Effect size of amygdala-NF: Two types of evidence are needed to support the premise of NF as an intervention tool for psychiatric disorders. 用NF作为精神疾病介入手段的2个前提

     1. (a) NF training should lead to a learned self-regulation of the targeted brain signal 训练应该要能引起被试自己调控的能力

     2. (b) such learning should result in a neurobehavioral or clinical change. 最终要引起神经行为或者临床症状的改变

  2. Design parameters and amygdala related processes: Several recent reviews highlighted the need for an organizing framework to guide design choices (Lubianiker et al., 2019, Paret et al., 2019, Thibault et al., 2018). Here, we tested whether the Research Domain Criteria (RDoC) framework (Cuthbert, 2014), and specifically the positive and negative valence systems (in both of which the amygdala is a key hub (Beyeler et al., 2018, O’Neill et al., 2018), could be used to explain experimental design choices in amygdala-targeting NF studies. For this, we summarized design parameters used in published amygdala-NF studies and examined if design choices correspond to the targeted valence domain (positive or negative).

  3. Neural mechanism of successful Amygdala self-regulation: Studies examining the neural mechanism of amygdala-related NF (e.g Keynan et al., 2019, Paret et al., 2016b, Paret et al., 2016a, Zotev et al., 2013) mostly analyzed entire cohorts without considering individual neuromodulation success. This precluded differentiation between general effort to exert modulation and learning processes. We used a cross-lab large amygdala-fMRI-NF data set while accounting for amygdala regulation success, to unveil the neural mechanism that underlies effective volitional neuromodulation of the amygdala.




  


Effect size 具体包括：#ToDo: 预实验数据计算

Three types of contrasts were evaluated for effect sizes either for within- or between-group effects (total of six): 

  1. Neuromodulation effect was defined as the difference between mean amygdala related activity during baseline and mean amygdala related activity during regulate conditions across all experimental sessions. The regulate condition refers to blocks where participants actively attempted to regulate the amygdala related signal. The baseline condition refers to blocks where participants were instructed not to perform modulation. This effect size was pooled from all included studies and refers to the test group only. The between-group effect size was based on the difference in neuromodulation between test and control groups. This analysis was performed only for studies including an active control condition (i.e. NF from a control region or shame signal).

  2. Learning effect was defined as the difference in Neuromodulation between the first and last NF session. Only studies that included more than one NF session were included in this analysis. The between-group effect size was based on the difference in learning between the test and control groups. This was only possible to calculate for studies that included more than one session and an active control condition.

  3. Clinical outcome effect was defined as the difference in the main clinical outcome post vs pre NF training, this index was pooled only for studies involving clinical population and included a clinical outcome. The between-group effect size was based on the differences in clinical outcome between the test and control groups. Control groups were either active or no-treatment.




  


  


Design parameters 包括：

  1. Population



  1. NF probe (right, left, bilateral amygdala BOLD or EFP),

  2. direction of regulation (up/down, both),

  3. number of sessions,

  4. interface,

  5. instructions (non-specific emotion regulation, cognitive reappraisal, positive memory recall, sad autobio graphic memories, no-instructions), 

  6. behavioral outcome measures (self report rating, clinical evaluation, behavioral task, no-outcome),

  7. sample size,

  8. control group,

  9. RDoC targeted valence system (positive or negative)

  10. type of baseline that was used to compute NF success (fixation vs stimuli).




  


  


seen in the lower panel of Fig. 2c, grouping studies by the targeted RDoC neurobehavioral domain (i.e. negative or positive valence systems) yielded significant results (domain X direction of regulation; χ2 = 25.4, p < 0.001, domain X NF probe; χ2 = 33.4, p < 0.001, domain X in structions; χ2 = 34.2, p < 0.001). 

  


Fig. 2 further shows that, as one would expect, in the majority of cases (80%) studies that target down regulation also target reduction of negative valance and that studies targeting amygdala up-regulation also target enhancement of positive valance. The studies in which the direction was not aligned with valance are studies that tested methodological issues such as continuous vs intermittent feedback (Hellrung et al., 2018b), or implicit vs explicit instructions (Marxen et al., 2016).
